# Probabilistic Sensor Fusion & State Estimation

A humanoid robot is an inherently underactuated, floating-base dynamical system. While joint motor encoders provide accurate relative kinematics between limbs, they cannot measure the robot's absolute 6-DOF pose in world coordinates. Relying exclusively on any single sensor modality is hazardous:
- **Cameras** experience optical blur during aggressive head sway and fail in dark or featureless corridors.
- **LiDARs** suffer from geometric degeneracy in long, featureless hallways and tunnel-like environments.
- **Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs)** suffer from unbounded double-integration quadratic drift.
- **Leg Kinematics (Dead Reckoning)** degrade when foot sole slip or viscoelastic ground compliance occurs.

**Probabilistic Multi-Sensor Fusion** provides a mathematically principled framework to unify complementary, asynchronously sampled sensor streams into a globally consistent state estimate. This chapter derives the **Kalman Filter family (KF, EKF, UKF)**, examines **Factor Graph Smoothing (iSAM2)**, details **tightly coupled LiDAR-Inertial-Visual Odometry (LIVO)**, and configures the ROS 2 **`robot_localization`** dual-EKF architecture.

## 1. Mathematical Derivations: The Kalman Filter Family

Let the robot state at discrete timestep $k$ be represented by $\mathbf{x}_k \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with process dynamics and sensor measurements governed by:

$$
\mathbf{x}_k = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{k-1}, \mathbf{u}_k) + \mathbf{w}_k, \quad \mathbf{w}_k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{Q}_k)
$$

$$
\mathbf{z}_k = \mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x}_k) + \mathbf{v}_k, \quad \mathbf{v}_k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{R}_k)
$$

where $\mathbf{Q}_k$ is process noise covariance and $\mathbf{R}_k$ is measurement noise covariance.

### 1.1 The Linear Kalman Filter (KF)

When $\mathbf{f}$ and $\mathbf{h}$ are linear operations ($\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{F}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}$, $\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}$), the optimal minimum mean-squared error estimator is given by:

1. **Time Update (Predict)**:
   $$
   \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1} = \mathbf{F}_k \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1|k-1} + \mathbf{B}_k \mathbf{u}_k
   $$
   $$
   \mathbf{P}_{k|k-1} = \mathbf{F}_k \mathbf{P}_{k-1|k-1} \mathbf{F}_k^T + \mathbf{Q}_k
   $$
2. **Measurement Update (Correct)**:
   $$
   \mathbf{S}_k = \mathbf{H}_k \mathbf{P}_{k|k-1} \mathbf{H}_k^T + \mathbf{R}_k \quad (\text{Innovation Covariance})
   $$
   $$
   \mathbf{K}_k = \mathbf{P}_{k|k-1} \mathbf{H}_k^T \mathbf{S}_k^{-1} \quad (\text{Optimal Kalman Gain})
   $$
   $$
   \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k} = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1} + \mathbf{K}_k (\mathbf{z}_k - \mathbf{H}_k \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1})
   $$
   $$
   \mathbf{P}_{k|k} = (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_k \mathbf{H}_k) \mathbf{P}_{k|k-1}
   $$

### 1.2 The Extended Kalman Filter (EKF)

For nonlinear systems, the EKF linearizes dynamics around the current state estimate via first-order Taylor expansion:

$$
\mathbf{F}_k = \left. \frac{\partial \mathbf{f}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} \right|_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1|k-1}}, \quad \mathbf{H}_k = \left. \frac{\partial \mathbf{h}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} \right|_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1}}
$$

While computationally lightweight, the EKF diverges when transformations are highly nonlinear (such as rotation group manifolds $SO(3)$ subject to large angular uncertainty) because first-order linearization introduces truncation bias.

### 1.3 The Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF)

The **Unscented Kalman Filter** (Julier & Uhlmann, 2004) avoids Jacobian differentiation by propagating a deterministic set of $2n + 1$ **sigma points** through the true nonlinear functions.

Given state dimension $n$, mean $\bar{\mathbf{x}}$, and covariance $\mathbf{P}$, the sigma points $\boldsymbol{\chi}_i$ are generated via matrix square root (Cholesky decomposition):

$$
\boldsymbol{\chi}_0 = \bar{\mathbf{x}}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\chi}_i = \bar{\mathbf{x}} + \left( \sqrt{(n + \lambda)\mathbf{P}} \right)_i, \quad i = 1, \dots, n
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{i+n} = \bar{\mathbf{x}} - \left( \sqrt{(n + \lambda)\mathbf{P}} \right)_i, \quad i = 1, \dots, n
$$

where scaling parameter $\lambda = \alpha^2 (n + \kappa) - n$. The corresponding mean and covariance weights are:

$$
W_0^{(m)} = \frac{\lambda}{n + \lambda}, \quad W_0^{(c)} = \frac{\lambda}{n + \lambda} + (1 - \alpha^2 + \beta)
$$

$$
W_i^{(m)} = W_i^{(c)} = \frac{1}{2(n + \lambda)}, \quad i = 1, \dots, 2n
$$

Each sigma point is propagated through the nonlinear transition: $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_i = \mathbf{f}(\boldsymbol{\chi}_i, \mathbf{u}_k)$. The predicted mean and covariance are reconstructed via weighted sums:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1} = \sum_{i=0}^{2n} W_i^{(m)} \boldsymbol{\gamma}_i, \quad \mathbf{P}_{k|k-1} = \sum_{i=0}^{2n} W_i^{(c)} (\boldsymbol{\gamma}_i - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1})(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_i - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1})^T + \mathbf{Q}_k
$$

The Unscented Transform achieves 3rd-order Taylor accuracy for Gaussian inputs, providing accurate covariance tracking without calculating analytical Jacobians.

## 2. Filtering vs. Smoothing: Factor Graph SLAM

Classical filters (EKF/UKF) maintain only the most recent state $\mathbf{x}_k$, marginalizing past states immediately. This incurs two fundamental drawbacks:
1. **Irreversible Linearization**: Errors introduced at step $k-50$ cannot be corrected when a loop closure is discovered at step $k$.
2. **Dense Fill-In**: Marginalizing intermediate landmark states creates dense covariance matrices, increasing computational cost.

**Factor Graph Smoothing** (Kaess et al., 2008) retains the history of states over a sliding window $\mathcal{X} = \{\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_N\}$ and solves a sparse nonlinear least-squares problem:

$$
\mathcal{X}^* = \arg\min_{\mathcal{X}} \left[ \sum_{i} \|\mathbf{r}_{\text{prior}}(\mathbf{x}_i)\|_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i}^2 + \sum_{k} \|\mathbf{r}_{\text{odom}}(\mathbf{x}_k, \mathbf{x}_{k+1})\|_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_k}^2 + \sum_{j} \|\mathbf{r}_{\text{meas}}(\mathbf{x}_j, \mathbf{z}_j)\|_{\boldsymbol{\Omega}_j}^2 \right]
$$

Using **iSAM2 (Incremental Smoothing and Mapping)** with the Bayes tree data structure, only variables affected by new measurements are re-linearized, achieving real-time performance on mobile hardware.

## 3. Consistency Verification: Normalized Innovation Squared (NIS)

To prevent filter divergence, the fusion engine must continuously monitor whether sensory measurements match predicted error covariances using the **Normalized Innovation Squared (NIS)** test:

$$
\epsilon_{k} = \mathbf{y}_k^T \mathbf{S}_k^{-1} \mathbf{y}_k \quad (\mathbf{y}_k = \mathbf{z}_k - \hat{\mathbf{z}}_k)
$$

Under consistent Gaussian assumptions, $\epsilon_k$ follows a chi-squared distribution with $m$ degrees of freedom (where $m = \dim(\mathbf{z})$):

$$
\epsilon_k \sim \chi_m^2
$$

If $\epsilon_k > \chi_{m, 0.95}^2$, the measurement is flagged as an outlier (e.g., LiDAR beam passing through glass or camera seeing dynamic reflections) and rejected before it can corrupt the filter state.

## 4. Production Dual-EKF Configuration: `robot_localization`

Industrial humanoid deployments utilize the ROS 2 `robot_localization` package structured into a two-tier cascaded architecture:

### 1. `ekf_local.yaml` (Continuous Odometry Filter)
Fuses high-rate proprioceptive sources: pelvis IMU ($1 \text{ kHz}$), leg odometry ($100 \text{ Hz}$), and cuVSLAM ($60 \text{ Hz}$). The output transform (`odom -> base_link`) is guaranteed to be **strictly continuous with zero sudden jumps**, protecting low-level 1 kHz bipedal whole-body controllers.

```yaml
ekf_filter_node_local:
  ros__parameters:
    frequency: 100.0
    two_d_mode: false
    publish_tf: true
    map_frame: map
    odom_frame: odom
    base_link_frame: base_link
    world_frame: odom

    # Sensor 1: Pelvis IMU (ICM-42688-P)
    imu0: /humanoid/sensors/imu/pelvis/data
    imu0_config: [false, false, false,   # x, y, z position
                  true,  true,  true,    # roll, pitch, yaw
                  false, false, false,   # vx, vy, vz
                  true,  true,  true,    # roll_rate, pitch_rate, yaw_rate
                  true,  true,  true]    # ax, ay, az
    imu0_differential: false
    imu0_relative: false
    imu0_queue_size: 20

    # Sensor 2: cuVSLAM Visual Odometry
    odom0: /visual_slam/tracking/odometry
    odom0_config: [true,  true,  true,   # x, y, z position
                   true,  true,  true,   # roll, pitch, yaw
                   true,  true,  true,   # vx, vy, vz
                   false, false, false,  # angular rates
                   false, false, false]  # linear accelerations
    odom0_differential: false
    odom0_relative: false
    odom0_queue_size: 10
```

### 2. `ekf_global.yaml` (Global Map Filter)
Fuses local odometry with absolute global references (e.g., LiDAR-Inertial LIO-SAM relocalization, AprilTag floor anchors, or RTK-GPS). The output transform (`map -> odom`) absorbs discrete drift corrections, maintaining global alignment without causing step discontinuities in the robot base controller.

## 5. Complete Python Consistency Verification & NIS Testing Harness

Below is a complete, production-grade Python script simulating a multi-sensor fusion pipeline, executing EKF state updates, and validating filter consistency using the chi-squared NIS test:

```python
"""
sensor_fusion_nis_verifier.py
Simulates an Extended Kalman Filter fusing IMU and visual odometry,
and verifies mathematical covariance consistency using Normalized Innovation Squared (NIS).
"""

from typing import Tuple, List
import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import chi2


class HumanoidStateEstimatorEKF:
    """
    6-DOF EKF fusing linear accelerations, angular rates, and position measurements.
    State vector: [p_x, p_y, p_z, v_x, v_y, v_z] (6x1)
    """

    def __init__(self, dt: float = 0.01):
        self.dt = dt
        self.dim_x = 6
        self.x = np.zeros((6, 1))  # State: [x, y, z, vx, vy, vz]
        self.P = np.eye(6) * 0.1   # Initial state covariance

        # State transition matrix F
        self.F = np.eye(6)
        self.F[0, 3] = dt
        self.F[1, 4] = dt
        self.F[2, 5] = dt

        # Process noise covariance Q
        q_pos = 0.001
        q_vel = 0.02
        self.Q = np.diag([q_pos, q_pos, q_pos, q_vel, q_vel, q_vel])

        # Measurement model H (observing positions [x, y, z] from cuVSLAM)
        self.H = np.zeros((3, 6))
        self.H[0, 0] = 1.0
        self.H[1, 1] = 1.0
        self.H[2, 2] = 1.0

        # Measurement noise covariance R
        self.R = np.eye(3) * (0.02 ** 2)

    def predict(self, accel_input: np.ndarray):
        """
        Predict step with control input u = accel (3x1):
        p += v * dt + 0.5 * a * dt^2,  v += a * dt
        """
        B = np.zeros((6, 3))
        B[0:3, :] = np.eye(3) * 0.5 * (self.dt ** 2)
        B[3:6, :] = np.eye(3) * self.dt

        self.x = self.F @ self.x + B @ accel_input
        self.P = self.F @ self.P @ self.F.T + self.Q

    def update(self, z: np.ndarray) -> Tuple[float, bool]:
        """
        Measurement update step. Returns (nis_value, is_consistent).
        """
        z_pred = self.H @ self.x
        y = z - z_pred  # Innovation

        S = self.H @ self.P @ self.H.T + self.R  # Innovation covariance
        S_inv = np.linalg.inv(S)

        K = self.P @ self.H.T @ S_inv  # Kalman Gain

        self.x = self.x + K @ y
        self.P = (np.eye(self.dim_x) - K @ self.H) @ self.P

        # Calculate Normalized Innovation Squared (NIS): y^T * S^-1 * y
        nis = float((y.T @ S_inv @ y).item())

        # Chi-squared test for 3 degrees of freedom at 95% confidence (threshold = 7.815)
        chi2_threshold = chi2.ppf(0.95, df=3)
        is_consistent = nis <= chi2_threshold

        return nis, is_consistent


if __name__ == "__main__":
    print("=" * 65)
    print("Multi-Sensor Fusion Consistency & NIS Verification")
    print("=" * 65)

    estimator = HumanoidStateEstimatorEKF(dt=0.01)
    nis_history: List[float] = []

    # Simulate 100 timesteps of forward bipedal motion
    true_pos = np.zeros(3)
    true_vel = np.array([0.6, 0.0, 0.0])

    outlier_count = 0
    total_steps = 100

    for step in range(total_steps):
        true_pos += true_vel * 0.01
        accel_meas = np.array([[0.0], [0.0], [0.0]])  # Constant velocity

        estimator.predict(accel_meas)

        # Simulate cuVSLAM position reading with Gaussian noise
        noise = np.random.normal(0, 0.02, 3)
        # Inject one artificial sensor glitch outlier at step 50
        if step == 50:
            noise += np.array([0.5, 0.0, 0.0])  # 50 cm visual tracking jump

        z_meas = (true_pos + noise).reshape(3, 1)

        nis, is_valid = estimator.update(z_meas)
        nis_history.append(nis)
        if not is_valid:
            outlier_count += 1

    chi2_upper = chi2.ppf(0.95, df=3)
    avg_nis = np.mean(nis_history)

    print(f"Total Steps Evaluated:          {total_steps}")
    print(f"Degrees of Freedom (Measurement): 3")
    print(f"Chi-Squared 95% Confidence Limit: {chi2_upper:.3f}")
    print(f"Empirical Average NIS:           {avg_nis:.3f} (Expected near 3.0)")
    print(f"Outliers Detected via Gate:      {outlier_count} / {total_steps}")

    if avg_nis < chi2_upper:
        print("[PASS] Filter covariance is statistically consistent with measurements.")
    else:
        print("[FAIL] Filter is overconfident or diverging.")
    print("=" * 65)
```

## 6. Summary & Seminal Literature

Multi-sensor fusion enables bipedal humanoid autonomy by combining complementary proprioceptive and exteroceptive sensors. By contrasting linear KFs, nonlinear EKFs, and derivative-free UKFs, and structuring edge computation into a dual-EKF architecture with chi-squared innovation consistency monitoring, roboticists achieve continuous, jump-free state feedback for real-time walking stabilization.

### Seminal Research Citations
1. **Julier, S. J., & Uhlmann, J. K. (2004).** *Unscented filtering and nonlinear estimation*. Proceedings of the IEEE, 92(3), 401–422.
2. **Kaess, M., Ranganathan, A., & Dellaert, F. (2008).** *iSAM: Incremental smoothing and mapping*. IEEE Transactions on Robotics, 24(6), 1365–1378.
3. **Shan, T., Englot, B., Meyers, D., Wang, W., Ratti, C., & Rus, D. (2020).** *LIO-SAM: Tightly-coupled lidar inertial odometry via smoothing and mapping*. IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS).

# Privileged Learning: Asymmetric Actor-Critic & RMA

In reinforcement learning, the environment simulator is not merely a generator of sample transitions—it is an omniscient digital oracle. Inside simulation, we possess instantaneous, noise-free access to ground-truth physical quantities: the exact friction coefficient $\mu$ under each foot sole, the precise millimeter-level terrain elevation map surrounding the robot, the center of mass displacement caused by a hidden payload, the instantaneous ground reaction force vectors, and motor winding resistance.

On physical humanoid hardware, virtually none of these variables can be measured directly. Equipping a robot with dense arrays of external LiDARs, load cells, and soil sensors adds weight, fragility, and computational latency. **Privileged Learning** resolves this asymmetry through a two-stage paradigm: first, we train an optimal "teacher" policy that leverages privileged simulation data; second, we distill this privileged intelligence into a "student" policy that runs purely on physical onboard proprioception (joint encoders, IMU, and recent action history).

## 1. Asymmetric Actor-Critic (AAC)

Standard Actor-Critic algorithms constrain both the policy $\pi_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{a}_t \mid \mathbf{s}_t)$ and the value critic $V_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{s}_t)$ to observe the identical state $\mathbf{s}_t$. In sim-to-real transfer, this constraint is unnecessarily restrictive: the value function is discarded after training and is **never executed on the physical robot**.

In **Asymmetric Actor-Critic (AAC)**, the critic is fed the full privileged state $\mathbf{S}_t^{\text{priv}} = (\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{e}_t)$, while the actor observes only real-world deployable observations $\mathbf{s}_t$:

$$
\text{Actor: } \pi_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{a}_t \mid \mathbf{s}_t), \quad \text{Critic: } V_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{e}_t)
$$

### The Value Variance Reduction Theorem
By conditioning the value function on privileged information $\mathbf{e}_t$, the environment appears deterministic rather than partially observable (POMDP $\to$ MDP). The variance of the Generalized Advantage Estimator (GAE) drops substantially:

$$
\operatorname{Var}\left( \hat{A}_t^{\text{priv}} \right) = \operatorname{Var}\left( \sum_{l=0}^\infty (\gamma \lambda)^l \delta_{t+l}^{\text{priv}} \right) \ll \operatorname{Var}\left( \hat{A}_t^{\text{proprio}} \right)
$$

where temporal difference error $\delta_t^{\text{priv}} = r_t + \gamma V_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{s}_{t+1}, \mathbf{e}_{t+1}) - V_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{e}_t)$ exhibits zero variance stemming from hidden physical parameters.

## 2. Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) Architecture

Introduced by Kumar et al. (2021), **Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA)** extends privileged learning into a two-stage framework that estimates environmental changes online from proprioception alone.

### Stage 1: Base Policy Training with Environmental Latents
Let $\mathbf{e}_t \in \mathbb{R}^E$ denote the privileged physics vector (e.g., foot friction $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^2$, payload mass $m \in \mathbb{R}$, center of mass shift $\Delta \mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, joint damping offsets). An environmental factor encoder $\mu_{\mathbf{e}}: \mathbb{R}^E \to \mathbb{R}^Z$ compresses this vector into a compact latent context $\mathbf{z}_t \in \mathbb{R}^8$:

$$
\mathbf{z}_t = \mu_{\mathbf{e}}(\mathbf{e}_t)
$$

The base control policy $\pi_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{a}_t \mid \mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{z}_t)$ is trained via model-free reinforcement learning (such as PPO) across thousands of parallel randomized environments. Because $\mathbf{z}_t$ informs the policy whether it is on ice or rubber, or carrying a light or heavy load, the policy learns specialized, highly agile dynamic primitives.

### Stage 2: Supervised Temporal Trajectory Distillation
Once the base policy $\pi_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ and encoder $\mu_{\mathbf{e}}$ converge, their weights are frozen. A physical robot cannot evaluate $\mu_{\mathbf{e}}(\mathbf{e}_t)$ because $\mathbf{e}_t$ is unknown. However, the physical interactions of the robot with its environment—such as foot slippage, actuator deceleration under load, and chassis tilt—are implicitly encoded within the **temporal history of proprioceptive states and actions**:

$$
\mathbf{H}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{s}_t & \mathbf{s}_{t-1} & \dots & \mathbf{s}_{t-K+1} \\ \mathbf{a}_{t-1} & \mathbf{a}_{t-2} & \dots & \mathbf{a}_{t-K} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times (D_s + D_a)}
$$

We train a student **Adaptation Module** $g_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\mathbf{H}_t)$ parameterized by a 1D Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) to predict the latent context $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_t$:

$$
\min_{\boldsymbol{\psi}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}(\boldsymbol{\psi}) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}} \left[ \|\hat{\mathbf{z}}_t - \mathbf{z}_t\|^2 \right] = \mathbb{E}_{\tau} \left[ \left\| g_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\mathbf{H}_t) - \mu_{\mathbf{e}}(\mathbf{e}_t) \right\|_2^2 \right]
$$

Because this is a supervised regression objective over transitions generated in simulation, training converges rapidly (within 10–20 minutes on a modern GPU) without reward engineering or policy divergence.

## 3. Visual Distillation: DreamWaQ & Privileged Terrain Elevation

While RMA focuses on dynamic contact parameters, locomotion over rough, unstructured terrain requires exteroceptive geometric awareness. Standard vision pipelines require real-time 3D point cloud registration, surface mesh reconstruction, and ray-casting, which introduce severe latency ($50\text{--}150\text{ ms}$) and sensor dropouts.

**DreamWaQ** (Nahrendra et al., 2023) and **PKD** (Privileged Knowledge Distillation) extend the privileged paradigm to vision:
1. In simulation, the teacher policy receives an exact digital elevation map (height scan grid $h(x, y)$ of $1.5\text{ m} \times 1.0\text{ m}$ sampled at $5\text{ cm}$ resolution around the robot's base).
2. The student policy receives a stream of raw, noisy depth images from an onboard RealSense D435 camera or purely proprioceptive terrain impact history.
3. The distillation loss combines latent feature matching with policy action KL divergence:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{vis}}(\boldsymbol{\psi}) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{s}_t} \left[ D_{\text{KL}}\left(\pi_{\text{teacher}}(\cdot \mid \mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{h}_{\text{grid}}) \,\Big\|\, \pi_{\text{student}}(\cdot \mid \mathbf{s}_t, \mathbf{I}_{\text{depth}}; \boldsymbol{\psi})\right) \right] + \lambda \|\mathbf{z}_{\text{student}} - \mathbf{z}_{\text{geom}}\|^2
$$

## 4. Comparison of Privileged Learning Paradigms

| Architecture | Privileged Inputs ($\mathbf{e}_t$) | Student Inputs ($\mathbf{s}_t$) | Distillation Target | Online Compute Overhead | Physical Reaction Latency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Asymmetric Actor-Critic (AAC)** | Full sim physics, forces, friction | Current proprioception $\mathbf{s}_t$ | None (Critic discarded at runtime) | $0.0\text{ ms}$ (No extra module) | Zero adaptation (Fixed blind policy) |
| **RMA (Kumar et al.)** | Friction, payload, CoM, damping | 50-step joint & IMU history $\mathbf{H}_t$ | Environmental latent $\mathbf{z}_t \in \mathbb{R}^8$ | $0.8\text{ ms}$ (1D TCN on Jetson) | $20\text{--}40\text{ ms}$ (2–4 gait steps) |
| **DreamWaQ** | Exact ground heightmap $h(x, y)$ | Proprioceptive history + velocity | 2D body height & foot clearance | $0.5\text{ ms}$ (MLP encoder) | $10\text{--}20\text{ ms}$ |
| **PKD (Privileged Knowledge)** | Object 6D pose, contact normal | Vision features + tactile skin | Teacher action distribution $q(a)$ | $3.5\text{ ms}$ (Vision transformer) | $30\text{--}60\text{ ms}$ |

## 5. Production PyTorch Implementation: Two-Stage RMA Pipeline

Below is a complete, production-grade PyTorch implementation of the RMA architecture, containing the privileged environment encoder, the base policy network, the 1D CNN trajectory history adaptation module, and the supervised distillation trainer:

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
rma_privileged_learning.py
Production-grade PyTorch implementation of Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA)
featuring the Privileged Environment Factor Encoder, Base Control Policy,
and Temporal Convolutional Student Adaptation Module.
"""

import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F
from typing import Tuple, Dict


class PrivilegedEnvironmentEncoder(nn.Module):
    """
    Stage 1 Teacher Component:
    Compresses high-dimensional privileged simulation physics vector e_t
    into an 8-dimensional environmental latent context z_t.
    """

    def __init__(self, priv_dim: int = 24, latent_dim: int = 8):
        super().__init__()
        self.encoder = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(priv_dim, 64),
            nn.ELU(),
            nn.Linear(64, 32),
            nn.ELU(),
            nn.Linear(32, latent_dim),
        )

    def forward(self, e_t: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        return self.encoder(e_t)


class RMABasePolicy(nn.Module):
    """
    Base Control Policy pi(a_t | s_t, z_t):
    Outputs Gaussian action means for PD motor controllers given the current
    proprioceptive state s_t and environment latent vector z_t.
    """

    def __init__(self, proprio_dim: int = 36, latent_dim: int = 8, action_dim: int = 12):
        super().__init__()
        self.net = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(proprio_dim + latent_dim, 256),
            nn.ELU(),
            nn.Linear(256, 128),
            nn.ELU(),
            nn.Linear(128, action_dim),
        )
        self.log_std = nn.Parameter(torch.zeros(action_dim))

    def forward(self, s_t: torch.Tensor, z_t: torch.Tensor) -> Tuple[torch.Tensor, torch.Tensor]:
        x = torch.cat([s_t, z_t], dim=-1)
        action_mean = self.net(x)
        action_std = torch.exp(self.log_std).expand_as(action_mean)
        return action_mean, action_std


class StudentAdaptationModule(nn.Module):
    """
    Stage 2 Student Component:
    1D Temporal Convolutional Network that processes a rolling history window
    H_t = [s_t-K:t, a_t-K:t-1] to reconstruct the environment latent z_hat_t.
    """

    def __init__(self, history_len: int = 50, feature_dim: int = 48, latent_dim: int = 8):
        super().__init__()
        # Input shape: (Batch, feature_dim, history_len)
        self.conv_block = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Conv1d(feature_dim, 32, kernel_size=5, stride=2),  # 50 -> 23
            nn.ELU(),
            nn.Conv1d(32, 32, kernel_size=5, stride=2),           # 23 -> 10
            nn.ELU(),
            nn.Conv1d(32, 32, kernel_size=3, stride=1),           # 10 -> 8
            nn.ELU(),
        )
        self.fc = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(32 * 8, 64),
            nn.ELU(),
            nn.Linear(64, latent_dim),
        )

    def forward(self, history: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        """
        Args:
            history: Tensor of shape (Batch, history_len, feature_dim)
        Returns:
            z_hat: Estimated latent of shape (Batch, latent_dim)
        """
        # Permute to (Batch, feature_dim, history_len) for Conv1d
        x = history.transpose(1, 2)
        feat = self.conv_block(x)
        feat = feat.flatten(start_dim=1)
        z_hat = self.fc(feat)
        return z_hat


class RMADistillationTrainer:
    """Manages Stage 2 supervised distillation training."""

    def __init__(
        self,
        student_module: StudentAdaptationModule,
        priv_encoder: PrivilegedEnvironmentEncoder,
        lr: float = 1e-3,
        device: str = "cpu",
    ):
        self.device = torch.device(device)
        self.student = student_module.to(self.device)
        self.priv_encoder = priv_encoder.to(self.device)
        # Freeze teacher encoder
        for p in self.priv_encoder.parameters():
            p.requires_grad = False

        self.optimizer = torch.optim.Adam(self.student.parameters(), lr=lr)

    def train_step(self, batch_history: torch.Tensor, batch_e_priv: torch.Tensor) -> float:
        """
        Executes one gradient descent step on supervised MSE loss ||z_hat - z||^2.
        """
        self.student.train()
        batch_history = batch_history.to(self.device)
        batch_e_priv = batch_e_priv.to(self.device)

        with torch.no_grad():
            target_z = self.priv_encoder(batch_e_priv)

        pred_z = self.student(batch_history)
        loss = F.mse_loss(pred_z, target_z)

        self.optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        self.optimizer.step()

        return float(loss.item())


if __name__ == "__main__":
    torch.manual_seed(42)

    # Dimensionality settings for a 12-DoF humanoid biped
    proprio_dim = 36   # 12 joint pos, 12 joint vel, 3 IMU gyro, 3 IMU accel, 6 base state
    action_dim = 12    # 12 joint position setpoints
    priv_dim = 24      # ground friction, payload mass, CoM offset, joint damping, motor gain errors
    latent_dim = 8
    history_len = 50   # 50 timesteps at 50 Hz = 1.0 second history window
    feature_dim = proprio_dim + action_dim  # 36 + 12 = 48

    # 1. Instantiate Stage 1 Components (Teacher)
    priv_encoder = PrivilegedEnvironmentEncoder(priv_dim=priv_dim, latent_dim=latent_dim)
    base_policy = RMABasePolicy(proprio_dim=proprio_dim, latent_dim=latent_dim, action_dim=action_dim)

    # 2. Instantiate Stage 2 Student Component
    student_module = StudentAdaptationModule(
        history_len=history_len, feature_dim=feature_dim, latent_dim=latent_dim
    )

    trainer = RMADistillationTrainer(student_module, priv_encoder, lr=1e-3)

    # 3. Simulate Supervised Distillation Training
    batch_size = 64
    print("Beginning Stage 2 Supervised Distillation Training...")
    for step in range(1, 6):
        # Synthetic batch sampled from rollout buffer
        dummy_history = torch.randn(batch_size, history_len, feature_dim)
        dummy_priv = torch.randn(batch_size, priv_dim)

        loss = trainer.train_step(dummy_history, dummy_priv)
        print(f"  Step {step}: Distillation MSE Loss = {loss:.6f}")

    # 4. Verify Real-World Deployment Inference
    student_module.eval()
    with torch.no_grad():
        test_history = torch.randn(1, history_len, feature_dim)
        test_proprio = torch.randn(1, proprio_dim)

        # Onboard execution: Student estimates latent, Base Policy outputs joint action
        z_hat = student_module(test_history)
        action_mean, _ = base_policy(test_proprio, z_hat)

        print("\n=== Real-World Deployment Inference Test ===")
        print(f"  Estimated Latent Vector z_hat shape: {list(z_hat.shape)}")
        print(f"  Commanded Joint Action shape:        {list(action_mean.shape)}")
        print(f"  Sample Action Output: {action_mean[0, :4].numpy().round(4)}")
```

## 6. Research Foundations & Seminal Literature

1. **Kumar, A., Fu, Z., Pathak, D., & Malik, J. (2021).** *RMA: Rapid Motor Adaptation for Legged Robots*. In Robotics: Science and Systems (RSS). [The foundational paper establishing two-stage privileged learning with temporal trajectory distillation for quadruped locomotion].
2. **Nahrendra, I. M. A., Yu, B., & Choi, H. (2023).** *DreamWaQ: Learning Robust Quadrupedal Locomotion With Implicit Terrain Imagination via Deep Reinforcement Learning*. IEEE Transactions on Robotics, 39(6), 4668-4682. [Introduces implicit visual heightmap distillation for rough terrain navigation without explicit elevation mapping].
3. **Pinto, L., Andrychowicz, M., Welinder, P., Zaremba, W., & Abbeel, P. (2017).** *Asymmetric Actor Critic for Image-Based Robot Learning*. In Robotics: Science and Systems (RSS). [Formulates asymmetric actor-critic where full simulator states train the value network while the policy operates solely on sensor observations].
4. **Chen, T., Kumar, A., Pathak, D., & Malik, J. (2020).** *Learning Complex Dexterous Manipulation with Privileged Information and Distillation*. arXiv:2010.02941. [Extends privileged distillation to multi-fingered dexterous hands manipulating varied object geometries].
5. **Miki, T., Lee, J., Hwangbo, J., Wellhausen, L., Koltun, V., & Hutter, M. (2022).** *Learning robust perceptive locomotion for quadrupedal robots in the wild*. Science Robotics, 7(62), eabk2822. [Demonstrates privileged visual and tactile distillation navigating mountain trails and snowy forests].
